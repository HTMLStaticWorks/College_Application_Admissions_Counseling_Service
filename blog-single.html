How to Find Your "Why" for the Common App

Dr. Elena Rodriguez

Oct 12, 2026 · 5 min read

Every year, I read thousands of essays that follow the exact same format: "I faced a challenge during [sports game/mission trip/difficult class], I worked really hard, and eventually I overcame it." While these are wonderful personal achievements, they rarely make for compelling college admissions essays.

The Problem with the "Resume Essay"

Your Common App activities list already tells the admissions committee what you do. Your transcript tells them how well you do academically. The essay is the only space where you can tell them why you do it, and who you are when you're doing it.

"The best essays don't try to impress. They try to connect."

Digging Deeper: The 3 "Why" Exercise

To find a topic that is uniquely yours, try the '3 Why' exercise. Take an activity you care about, and ask yourself why you do it three times:

  • Why do I debate? Because I like arguing.
  • Why do I like arguing? Because I enjoy looking at problems from multiple perspectives.
  • Why does that matter to me? Because growing up in a bilingual household, I constantly had to translate not just words, but cultural contexts.

Suddenly, an essay about debate becomes an essay about translation, perspective, and cultural empathy. That is an essay that stands out.
